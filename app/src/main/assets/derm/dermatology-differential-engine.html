Dermatology differential engine

Shortlist · Discriminate · Confirm

Enter what you can see. The tool ranks a fixed candidate set for that morphology, tells you which single observation would separate the leaders, and gives the test that would settle it. It ranks. It does not diagnose.

What this is. A structured reasoning aid for clinicians. Base rates are conditional on the presenting morphology, not on the population; percentages are proportions of the listed candidate set and will not match published prevalence. Likelihood ratios graded C are calibrated estimates derived from descriptive text and clinical convention, not measured values — they are directionally reliable and numerically soft, and should be adjusted to local case mix.

What it is not. It is not a diagnosis, not a substitute for examination and histopathology, and not complete. A diagnosis absent from a candidate set stays absent however the numbers move. Red flags run before the arithmetic because they outrank it.

Sources. The ranking engine — base rates, discriminating findings, likelihood ratios and confirmatory tests — is derived from Fitzpatrick's Clinical Dermatology, 8th edition, with guideline references cited where they apply. The Deeper reading layers beneath each candidate (dermoscopy or trichoscopy, histopathology, systemic associations and workup, and first-line management) are drawn from Andrews' Diseases of the Skin: Clinical Dermatology and reflect standard US terminology and practice. Content last checked against source: . Review cycle: 12 months.

On management. First-line management lines are an orientation to initial therapy, not a prescription. Doses, contraindications, monitoring and pregnancy considerations are deliberately omitted; drug choice and dosing must be confirmed against current local guidance and the individual patient before any treatment is given.

Not a medical device. The Dermatology Differential Engine gives structured decision support for education and clinical reasoning. It is not a medical device, has not been validated or cleared by any regulatory authority, and must not be used as the sole basis for diagnosis or treatment.
